## 0. Database philosophy

This volume is designed as the foundation of a machine-readable or human-reference Jyotiṣa conjunction database. It separates five questions that are often mixed together:

1. **What is the planetary relationship?**
2. **Where does it operate?**
3. **How does the sign modify it?**
4. **What functional jobs do the planets perform for this Lagna?**
5. **When is the combination activated?**

Phaladīpikā Chapter 18 explicitly covers the 21 two-planet combinations formed from the seven classical planets and instructs that when more than two planets occupy one house, the effects of the several possible pairs should be combined.

Bṛhat Jātaka also has a dedicated chapter on combinations of planets.

### Classical source mapping

Phaladīpikā Chapter 18 gives direct conjunction results in this sequence: Sun with each of the other six planets; Moon with the other five; Mars with the other four; Mercury with Jupiter/Venus/Saturn; Jupiter with Venus/Saturn; and Venus with Saturn.

## 10. Pair × House × Sign — 3,024-record engine

A complete matrix is generated by combining the preceding 21 pair definitions, 12 Bhāva definitions and 12 Rāśi definitions. Rather than pretending each combination is a separate classical quotation, the database uses a compositional rule:

```text
PAIR_THEME
+ HOUSE_DOMAIN
+ SIGN_MODIFIER
+ DISPOSITOR
= SYNTHETIC_INTERPRETATION
```

### Example record

`P13 | Mars–Mercury | H10 | Virgo`

- Pair: action + intellect/strategy.
- House: profession, authority, reputation.
- Sign: Virgo; analysis, precision, service, systems.
- Dispositor: Mercury.
- Required next checks: Mercury dignity, Mars dignity, aspects, combustion, Navāṃśa, Daśā.

## 18. Timing engine

### Primary activation candidates

2. Antardaśā of planet B
3. Mahādaśā of planet B
4. Antardaśā of planet A

The database should record timing separately from natal promise so that the system does not confuse **potential** with **activation**.

## 19. Source hierarchy

For research-quality implementation, use this priority:

**Tier 1 — Direct classical rule**  
A specific conjunction/house/sign rule stated in a primary classical text.

**Tier 2 — Classical synthesis**  
A rule derived by combining several explicit classical principles.

**Tier 3 — Traditional commentary**  
Later commentators explain or qualify a classical rule.

**Tier 4 — Modern synthesis**  
Contemporary interpretive language used to translate historical symbolism into modern occupations/life contexts.

Every database record should carry a `source_tier` field.

## 24. Research limitations

The classical literature is not internally uniform in every detail. Different texts, editions, translations and later traditions may use different thresholds or interpretations. This database therefore needs a **tradition/version field** when implementing disputed technical rules.

For example, planetary-war and conjunction classifications require astronomical degree information and should not be approximated by merely checking whether two planets occupy the same sign. Bṛhat Saṃhitā treats Graha Yuddha as a distinct technical subject.
