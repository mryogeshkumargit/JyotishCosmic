# Vedic Astrology (Jyotisha) — Comprehensive Guide to Yogas

**Research edition:** 2 October 2026  
**Scope:** Classical Jyotisha / Vedic astrology, with emphasis on *Bṛhat
Parāśara Horā Śāstra (BPHS)*, *Bṛhat Jātaka*, *Phaladīpikā*, and
*Sārāvalī*.

> **Important:** "How many Yogas are there?" does not have one
> universally correct number. Different classical texts organize and
> name Yoga systems differently, and later authors add further
> combinations. This guide therefore gives **families, classical counts
> where a text explicitly provides one, and a practical reference
> list**, rather than pretending that every tradition has one fixed
> master number.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. What is a Yoga in Vedic astrology?

The Sanskrit word **Yoga (योग)** broadly means a connection,
combination, conjunction, or configuration. In Jyotisha, a Yoga is a
specified relationship among planets (*grahas*), signs (*rāśis*), houses
(*bhāvas*), house lords, aspects (*dṛṣṭi*), or planetary strength.

A Yoga may be created through:

-   conjunction (*yuti*) of planets;
-   mutual or relevant planetary aspects;
-   planets occupying particular houses from Lagna or Moon;
-   relationships between house lords;
-   exchange of signs (*parivartana*);
-   occupation of Kendras (1, 4, 7, 10) and Trikonas (1, 5, 9);
-   exaltation, own sign, debilitation, combustion and other dignity
    conditions;
-   distribution of the seven classical planets across signs/houses;
-   relationships involving the Sun or Moon;
-   combinations repeated or modified in divisional charts;
-   special cancellation or reversal conditions.

A Yoga is therefore **not simply "two planets together."** A conjunction
can be one ingredient in a Yoga, but many Yogas are based on house-lord
relationships or planetary distribution rather than conjunction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Is there a fixed number of Yogas?

## Short answer: No.

There are several reasons.

### 2.1 Classical texts use different classifications

For example, the **Nābhasa Yoga** system is traditionally described as
**32 Yogas**, divided into:

| Family | Classical count |
|---|---|
| Āśraya Yogas | 3 |
| Dala Yogas | 2 |
| Ākṛti Yogas | 20 |
| Saṃkhya Yogas | 7 |
| **Total** | **32** |

The *Bṛhat Jātaka* and later classical traditions preserve this 32-fold
classification. A Sanskrit lexicographical source likewise describes
Nābhasa Yogas as 32, in four groups: 3 Āśraya, 2 Dala, 20 Ākṛti and 7
Saṃkhya.

### 2.2 Parāśara has many additional Yoga chapters

The BPHS corpus has dedicated chapters for:

-   Nābhasa Yogas;
-   manifold Yogas;
-   Moon Yogas;
-   Sun Yogas;
-   Rāja Yogas;
-   royal-patronage Yogas;
-   Dhana Yogas;
-   Dāridrya Yogas;
-   Mahāpuruṣa Yogas;
-   Pravrajyā/Renunciation Yogas;
-   and other combinations.

Thus the 32 Nābhasa Yogas are **not the total number of Yogas in
Jyotisha**.

### 2.3 Later texts add their own combinations

Works such as *Phaladīpikā*, *Sārāvalī*, *Jātaka Pārijāta*, *Hora Ratna*
and other manuals preserve, modify, rename, or expand combinations.

### 2.4 Some "Yogas" are families rather than single configurations

For example:

-   Raja Yoga is a broad family.
-   Dhana Yoga is a broad family.
-   Viparīta Raja Yoga is a family of reversals.
-   Parivartana Yoga can be classified into different subtypes.
-   Nābhasa Yoga itself contains 32 named configurations.

Therefore a statement such as **"there are exactly 300 Yogas"** should
be treated cautiously unless the author specifies the text, counting
method, and whether subtypes are included.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. The most useful way to classify Yogas

A practical classical classification is:

1.  **Nābhasa Yogas** — planetary distribution patterns.
2.  **Mahāpuruṣa Yogas** — five powerful planetary Yogas.
3.  **Chandra/Moon Yogas** — configurations around the Moon.
4.  **Surya/Sun Yogas** — configurations around the Sun.
5.  **Rāja Yogas** — combinations associated traditionally with
    authority, status and accomplishment.
6.  **Dhana Yogas** — wealth/resource combinations.
7.  **Daridra/Dāridrya Yogas** — poverty/resource obstruction
    combinations.
8.  **Viparīta Yogas** — difficult-house relationships that can
    produce reversal or gains through adversity.
9.  **Parivartana Yogas** — sign exchanges.
10. **Neecha-bhanga combinations** — cancellation/modification of
    debilitation.
11. **Adhi and other special Yogas** — specific positional
    configurations.
12. **Arishta and Arishta-bhanga** — affliction and
    cancellation/protection combinations.
13. **Pravrajya/Sannyasa Yogas** — renunciation/withdrawal
    combinations.
14. **Other subject-specific Yogas** — education, children, marriage,
    profession, fame, etc.

# 4. The 32 Nābhasa Yogas

Nābhasa Yogas are among the most systematic Yoga classifications in
classical Jyotisha. They depend primarily on **the distribution of the
seven traditional planets across signs/houses**, rather than on a single
planet's individual nature.

The four families are:

## 4.1 Āśraya Yogas — 3

These are based on the signs occupied by the seven planets.

### 1. Rajju Yoga

The planets occupy movable signs.

Traditional interpretations associate it with movement, travel, mobility
and changing circumstances.

### 2. Musala Yoga

The planets occupy fixed signs.

Traditionally associated with firmness, stability, persistence and
status.

### 3. Nala Yoga

The planets occupy dual signs.

Traditionally associated with adaptability, mixed circumstances and
practical intelligence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.2 Dala Yogas — 2

### 4. Māla Yoga

Benefic planets dominate the relevant Kendras in the specified classical
arrangement.

Traditionally associated with comforts, prosperity and pleasant
circumstances.

### 5. Sarpa Yoga

Malefic planets dominate the relevant Kendras in the specified
arrangement.

Traditionally associated with struggle, tension and difficult
circumstances.

**Important:** Exact formation rules should be checked against the
particular classical text being followed because traditions differ in
technical details.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are named after shapes or structural arrangements formed by
planetary distribution.

## 6. Gada Yoga

Planets concentrated in two adjacent Kendras.

## 12. Yava Yoga

## 13. Kamala / Padma Yoga

Planets distributed through the four Kendras.

## 14. Vapi Yoga

Planets concentrated in specified Panaphara or Apoklima groupings.

## 15. Yupa Yoga

A pillar/post-like arrangement.

## 16. Iṣu / Sara Yoga

An arrow-like arrangement.

## 17. Śakti Yoga

A spear/weapon-like distribution.

## 18. Daṇḍa Yoga

A staff-like distribution.

## 19. Nauka Yoga

A boat-like distribution.

## 20. Kūṭa Yoga

A peak/fort-like arrangement.

## 21. Chatra Yoga

An umbrella-like arrangement.

## 22. Chāpa / Karmuka Yoga

A bow-like arrangement.

## 23. Ardha-Chandra Yoga

A crescent-like distribution.

## 24. Chakra Yoga

A wheel-like distribution.

## 25. Samudra Yoga

An ocean-like distribution.

# 6. The 7 Saṃkhya Yogas

These depend on the **number of signs occupied** by the seven planets.

| Yoga | Number of occupied signs |
|---|---|
| Veena | 7 |
| Dāma | 6 |
| Pāśa | 5 |
| Kedāra | 4 |
| Śūla | 3 |
| Yuga | 2 |
| Gola | 1 |

The precise naming and ordering should be checked against the
edition/text being used; classical sources agree on the sevenfold
numerical family but translations sometimes vary in transliteration.

# 7. The Five Mahāpuruṣa Yogas

These are among the best-known Yogas in Vedic astrology.

A Mahāpuruṣa Yoga occurs when **Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus or
Saturn** is in a Kendra from Lagna (and in some traditions from Moon)
while occupying its **own sign or exaltation sign**.

| Yoga | Planet | Classical condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Ruchaka** | Mars | Mars in Kendra in own/exaltation sign |
| **Bhadra** | Mercury | Mercury in Kendra in own/exaltation sign |
| **Hamsa** | Jupiter | Jupiter in Kendra in own/exaltation sign |
| **Malavya** | Venus | Venus in Kendra in own/exaltation sign |
| **Śaśa / Sasa** | Saturn | Saturn in Kendra in own/exaltation sign |

### Ruchaka

Associated traditionally with courage, physical strength, command,
initiative and martial qualities.

### Bhadra

Associated with intellect, communication, learning, administration and
analytical ability.

### Hamsa

Associated traditionally with wisdom, ethics, learning, dignity and
prosperity.

### Malavya

Associated with beauty, comforts, relationships, vehicles, refinement
and material enjoyment.

### Sasa

Associated with organization, authority, endurance, administration and
influence.

**Strength matters:** A planet technically satisfying the basic rule can
still be modified by combustion, severe affliction, weakness in relevant
Vargas, or other chart factors.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Moon Yogas

## 8.1 Sunapha Yoga

One or more planets other than the Sun occupy the **2nd house from the
Moon**.

Traditional results include self-acquired resources, intelligence,
reputation and initiative.

## 8.2 Anapha Yoga

One or more planets occupy the **12th from the Moon**, excluding the
Sun.

Traditionally associated with independence, reputation, strength and
refinement.

## 8.3 Durudhara Yoga

Planets occupy **both 2nd and 12th from the Moon**.

Traditionally associated with resources, comforts, support and capacity
for sustained activity.

## 8.4 Kemadruma Yoga

In the basic classical formulation, the 2nd and 12th from Moon are
empty.

However, classical texts also give **cancellation conditions**.
Therefore it is incorrect to inspect only the two houses and immediately
declare that a person has a severe Kemadruma condition.

This is one of the most commonly over-simplified Yogas on modern
astrology websites.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Sun Yogas

Three principal configurations are traditionally described around the
Sun:

## 9.1 Vesi Yoga

Planets other than the Moon occupy the **2nd from the Sun**.

## 9.2 Vasi Yoga

Planets other than the Moon occupy the **12th from the Sun**.

## 9.3 Ubhayachari Yoga

Planets occupy **both 2nd and 12th from the Sun**.

These are further qualified according to whether the planets are benefic
or malefic.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Raja Yogas

"Raja Yoga" is best understood as a **family of combinations**, not one
single Yoga.

The classical principle is the relationship between important **Kendra
lords** and **Trikona lords**, especially the 5th and 9th lords.

### Key houses

| House | Traditional significance |
|---|---|
| 1st | self, identity, life direction |
| 4th | foundations, property, inner stability |
| 5th | intelligence, creativity, merit, children |
| 7th | partnerships, public dealings |
| 9th | fortune, dharma, teachers, higher principles |
| 10th | profession, action, status |

A particularly important principle is the relationship between the **9th
lord (dharma)** and **10th lord (karma)**.

This is often called **Dharma-Karmādhipati Yoga**.

Possible mechanisms include:

-   conjunction;
-   mutual aspect;
-   sign exchange;
-   placement in Kendras/Trikonas;
-   or other classical relationships depending on the text.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Dharma-Karmādhipati Yoga

This is formed through a significant relationship between the **9th lord
and 10th lord**.

Traditional interpretation associates it with the joining of:

-   **Dharma** — principles, fortune, higher purpose;
-   **Karma** — action, profession, public responsibility.

The result is traditionally interpreted as potential for recognition,
responsibility, professional achievement or influence.

However, the exact strength depends on:

-   dignity of both lords;
-   houses occupied;
-   aspects;
-   conjunctions;
-   combustion;
-   retrogression;
-   functional benefic/malefic status;
-   Navamsha and other Vargas;
-   relevant Dasha periods.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Dhana Yogas

Dhana means wealth.

Classical Dhana Yoga analysis usually pays special attention to:

-   **2nd house** — accumulated wealth/family resources;
-   **11th house** — gains/income;
-   **5th house** — speculation/intelligence/creative output;
-   **9th house** — fortune;
-   **Lagna** — capacity to utilize opportunities.

Important relationships include:

-   2nd lord with 11th lord;
-   2nd lord with 5th or 9th lord;
-   5th lord with 11th lord;
-   9th lord with 11th lord;
-   strong Lagna lord connected with wealth houses;
-   strong benefics supporting these houses.

A Dhana Yoga should not be interpreted as a guaranteed bank balance. It
describes a **configuration of wealth-producing potential under the
Jyotisha framework**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Daridra / Dāridrya Yogas

These are combinations traditionally associated with poverty, financial
difficulty or resource obstruction.

Classical rules can involve:

-   weakness of the 2nd lord;
-   weakness or affliction of the 11th;
-   wealth lords placed in difficult houses;
-   severe malefic influence;
-   simultaneous weakness of relevant houses and their lords.

Modern interpretation should avoid assuming that one isolated condition
means lifelong poverty. The complete chart and timing factors matter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Viparīta Raja Yoga

"Viparīta" means reversal.

This family generally concerns relationships among the lords of the
**6th, 8th and 12th houses**, traditionally called the Dusthanas.

Three commonly cited forms are:

### 14.1 Harsha Yoga

6th lord placed in a difficult house under specified conditions.

### 14.2 Sarala Yoga

8th lord placed in a difficult house under specified conditions.

### 14.3 Vimala Yoga

12th lord placed in a difficult house under specified conditions.

The underlying idea is that a difficult house lord entering another
difficult house can create a form of **reversal**, where one difficulty
becomes a source of resilience, advantage or improvement.

Different texts and modern schools apply somewhat different technical
rules.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Neecha Bhanga and Neecha Bhanga Raja Yoga

**Neecha** means debilitation.

A debilitated planet does not automatically mean failure.

Classical texts give various conditions by which the effects of
debilitation may be reduced or cancelled.

Examples of cancellation principles can involve:

-   the dispositor of the debilitation sign being strongly placed;
-   the lord of the planet's exaltation sign having a specified
    relationship;
-   relevant planets occupying Kendras;
-   the debilitated planet receiving appropriate support.

The phrase **Neecha Bhanga Raja Yoga** is widely used in modern Jyotisha
for stronger forms of cancellation associated with rise after
difficulty.

### Important caution

Not every debilitated planet produces a full "Neecha Bhanga Raja Yoga."
The exact cancellation rule and strength should be verified rather than
using a loose internet definition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Gaja Kesari Yoga

One of the most famous modern Jyotisha Yogas.

The standard formulation involves **Jupiter in a Kendra from the Moon**.

Classical results traditionally describe intelligence, reputation,
dignity, courage and capacity for achievement.

However, the strength of the result depends heavily on:

-   Jupiter's dignity;
-   Moon's dignity;
-   affliction;
-   combustion/association issues;
-   house placement;
-   aspects;
-   Navamsha;
-   Dasha.

Therefore simply seeing "Jupiter 1st/4th/7th/10th from Moon" is not
enough for a complete interpretation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Adhi Yoga

A classical formulation places benefic planets in specified houses from
the Moon, commonly the **6th, 7th and 8th from Moon**.

Traditional texts associate it with authority, influence and prosperity,
subject to planetary strength.

Different schools apply variations concerning:

-   natural benefics;
-   functional benefics;
-   malefic influence;
-   Lagna-based versus Moon-based calculation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Amala Yoga

A common formulation:

**A benefic planet in the 10th house from Lagna or Moon.**

Traditional results include:

-   good reputation;
-   virtuous conduct;
-   useful/publicly appreciated work;
-   prosperity.

Again, planetary strength and affliction modify the result.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Lakshmi Yoga

Lakshmi Yoga is traditionally associated with prosperity and fortune.

A common formulation involves:

-   strong Lagna lord;
-   strong 9th lord;
-   the 9th lord in own/exalted/favourable dignity;
-   and supportive Kendra/Trikona conditions.

Because different texts give slightly different formulations, the exact
rule should always be stated when diagnosing it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Saraswati Yoga

Traditionally associated with:

-   learning;
-   eloquence;
-   scholarship;
-   arts;
-   intellectual ability.

Classical formulations involve strength and favourable placement of
**Jupiter, Venus and Mercury**, especially in Kendras/Trikonas or
own/exaltation signs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. Budha-Aditya Yoga

This occurs when **Sun and Mercury are together**.

Traditional interpretations associate it with intelligence,
communication, administration and analytical ability.

### Critical qualification

A Sun-Mercury conjunction is common because Mercury never travels far
from the Sun.

Therefore **Budha-Aditya Yoga should not automatically be treated as an
extraordinary Yoga**.

Mercury's combustion, dignity, house placement, aspects and overall
strength materially change the interpretation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. Chandra-Mangala Yoga

Moon and Mars are connected by conjunction or other specified
relationship in traditions that recognize this Yoga.

It is traditionally associated with:

-   initiative;
-   financial activity;
-   enterprise;
-   emotional drive;
-   resource generation.

It can also indicate emotional intensity, so interpretation depends on
the Moon's condition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23. Lakshmi-type and wealth combinations

Many wealth Yogas are not one universally standardized formula.

A useful approach is to inspect combinations among:

**2nd + 5th + 9th + 11th + Lagna**

and then evaluate:

1.  house strength;
2.  lord strength;
3.  benefic/malefic influence;
4.  dignity;
5.  Dasha activation;
6.  transits.

This prevents "Yoga hunting," where every favourable-looking conjunction
is given a dramatic name.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. Parivartana Yoga

**Parivartana** means exchange.

Example:

-   Lord of Aries occupies Taurus.
-   Lord of Taurus occupies Aries.

The two planets have exchanged signs.

Classical traditions distinguish types of exchange based on the houses
involved.

A commonly used classification is:

### Maha Parivartana

Exchange involving auspicious/non-difficult houses.

### Khala Parivartana

Exchange involving the 3rd house in specified classifications.

### Dainya Parivartana

Exchange involving difficult houses such as 6th, 8th or 12th under
specified rules.

The exact taxonomy varies by text.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. Kartari Yogas

"Cartari" refers to being hemmed between planets.

## Subha Kartari Yoga

Benefics on both sides of a house/Lagna.

## Papa Kartari Yoga

Malefics on both sides.

The principle can be applied to:

-   Lagna;
-   a Bhava;
-   or, in some interpretive systems, other reference points.

It modifies the condition of what lies between the planets.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. Mahabhagya Yoga

A classical special configuration involving:

-   Sun;
-   Moon;
-   Lagna;
-   odd/even signs;
-   and day/night birth.

A common traditional formulation is:

**For a male born during daytime:** Sun, Moon and Lagna in odd signs.

**For a female born during nighttime:** Sun, Moon and Lagna in even
signs.

The exact gender/day-night formulation is a historical rule and should
be reported as such rather than presented as a universal modern
principle.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Vasumati Yoga

A wealth/prosperity-related Yoga associated with benefic planets
occupying favourable **Upachaya** positions from Lagna or Moon in
specified formulations.

The relevant Upachaya houses are:

-   3rd;
-   6th;
-   10th;
-   11th.

Classical rules vary in whether all or certain benefics must
participate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. Parvata Yoga

A classical prosperity/status combination involving the Lagna and house
lords under specified Kendra/Trikona and strength conditions.

Different texts describe somewhat different formation rules.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. Kahala Yoga

A classical Yoga involving relationships between:

-   Lagna lord;
-   relevant sign lord;
-   Kendras/Trikonas;
-   and planetary strength.

Traditional results include courage, authority and social standing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30. Sankha Yoga

Classical Sankha Yoga is associated with favourable relationships among
Kendra/Trikona lords and strong planetary support.

Traditional results include learning, morality, prosperity and
leadership.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 31. Bheri Yoga

A classical prosperity/comfort Yoga involving strong relationships
among:

-   Lagna;
-   2nd;
-   7th;
-   9th;
-   and Venus/Jupiter-type benefic factors depending on the text.

Traditional results include wealth, comforts, reputation and enjoyment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 32. Chamara Yoga

A classical Yoga associated with learning, eloquence, leadership and
recognition.

It generally requires strong benefic/royal combinations involving the
Lagna or Lagna lord.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 33. Matsya Yoga

A classical configuration with specified benefic and malefic placements
around the chart.

It is part of the larger set of named structural Yogas found in
classical literature.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 34. Kurma Yoga

A classical structural Yoga associated with strong, stable planetary
placement.

Traditional interpretations include authority, wealth, virtue and social
standing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 35. Devendra Yoga

A special classical combination associated with prosperity,
attractiveness, authority and enjoyment.

The exact formation should be checked against the particular source
because later manuals give differing rules.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 36. Indra Yoga

Another classical named combination, with formulations varying among
texts.

Traditional interpretations connect it with authority, courage, wealth
and recognition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 37. Chatussagara Yoga

A classical configuration involving strong planets in the four Kendras.

The name literally evokes the "four oceans."

Traditional results include fame, wealth, authority and social
prominence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 38. Hamsa and the distinction between Yoga families

It is important not to confuse:

-   **Hamsa Mahāpuruṣa Yoga**, produced by Jupiter's specific Kendra +
    dignity condition;

with every other occurrence of Jupiter in a Kendra.

A named Yoga has a **specific formation rule**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 39. Pravrajya / Sannyasa Yogas

These combinations are traditionally associated with:

-   renunciation;
-   withdrawal from ordinary material life;
-   ascetic tendencies;
-   intense spiritual practice.

Classical texts consider multiple planets and planetary strength.

Modern interpretation should not equate these automatically with literal
monkhood. A modern manifestation may be:

-   spiritual study;
-   research;
-   solitary professional work;
-   reduced attachment to material life;
-   institutional/religious service;
-   or actual renunciation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 40. Arishta Yogas

**Arishta** means affliction or adverse indication.

Classical Jyotisha contains combinations involving:

-   weak Lagna;
-   afflicted Moon;
-   difficult house placements;
-   severe malefic influence;
-   weak house lords;
-   and other factors.

These rules historically include some very severe interpretations
concerning health and longevity.

They should **not** be used as standalone modern medical predictions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 41. Arishta-bhanga

Just as classical astrology describes afflictions, it describes
**cancellation/protection**.

Benefic strength, a strong Lagna, strong Moon, Jupiter's influence and
other protective combinations can modify or cancel difficult
indications.

This is an important general Jyotisha principle:

> A chart should be read as a network of simultaneous indications, not
> as a collection of isolated labels.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 42. Why one person can have many Yogas

Suppose a hypothetical chart has:

-   Jupiter in a Kendra in own sign;
-   Mercury in a Kendra in own sign;
-   Moon with planets on both sides;
-   9th and 10th lords connected;
-   strong 2nd and 11th lords;
-   and planets distributed in a Nābhasa pattern.

That single horoscope could technically contain:

-   a Mahāpuruṣa Yoga;
-   a Moon Yoga;
-   a Raja Yoga;
-   one or more Dhana Yogas;
-   a Nābhasa Yoga;
-   and additional special combinations.

This is why "How many Yogas does this person have?" can produce a
surprisingly large number.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 43. Does having more Yogas mean a better horoscope?

**No.**

Counting Yogas is not a valid substitute for chart synthesis.

Five weak Yogas do not necessarily outweigh one very strong
configuration.

A Yoga must be evaluated for:

### A. Existence

Does the exact classical condition actually exist?

### B. Strength

Are the planets strong?

### C. Affliction

Are they heavily afflicted?

### D. House relevance

What houses do the planets rule and occupy?

### E. Dignity

Are they exalted, own-sign, friendly, neutral, enemy or debilitated?

### F. Vargas

Does the configuration retain strength in relevant divisional charts?

### G. Timing

Is the relevant planet operating its Dasha/Antardasha?

### H. Transits

Are current transits supporting activation?

### I. Cancellation

Is the Yoga weakened or cancelled?

### J. Repetition

Does the same theme appear independently elsewhere?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 44. Why a Yoga may not produce its textbook result

A classical Yoga can be present but weakly expressed.

Common reasons include:

1.  weak planet;
2.  combust planet;
3.  debilitated planet;
4.  severe malefic association;
5.  difficult house placement;
6.  weak dispositor;
7.  poor Navamsha;
8.  weak relevant Bhava;
9.  weak Bhava lord;
10. incompatible Dasha;
11. cancellation conditions;
12. competing stronger Yogas;
13. Yoga occurring only from one reference point;
14. different classical definitions.

Therefore:

**Yoga formation ≠ guaranteed event.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 45. The importance of Dasha

The **Vimshottari Dasha** system is particularly important in mainstream
Parashari practice.

A Yoga may exist throughout the birth chart, but its results are
traditionally expected to become more visible when:

-   a participating planet runs its Mahadasha;
-   a participating planet runs its Antardasha;
-   a relevant house lord is activated;
-   a connected planet is activated.

This is one reason two people with similar Yoga patterns can experience
them at very different ages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 46. The role of transits

Transits (*gochara*) are commonly used as an additional timing layer.

For example, astrologers may examine:

-   Jupiter's transit;
-   Saturn's transit;
-   Rahu/Ketu;
-   transit over natal planets;
-   transit through relevant houses;
-   transit relative to Moon/Lagna.

A natal Yoga is therefore generally interpreted as **potential**, while
Dasha and transit techniques are used for timing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 47. Lagna versus Moon: why the same Yoga can change

Many Yogas can be evaluated:

-   from Lagna;
-   from Moon;
-   and sometimes from other reference points.

For example, a benefic may be in a Kendra from Moon but not from Lagna.

That does not necessarily mean one calculation is "wrong."

The reference point changes the meaning:

**Lagna → body, life circumstances, identity and concrete
manifestation**

**Moon → mind, experience, perception, public/emotional dimension**

Thus a Yoga from Moon can be particularly relevant to psychological or
experiential expression, while the same configuration from Lagna may be
more directly tied to circumstances.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 48. Natural versus functional benefics

A planet can be:

-   naturally benefic;
-   naturally malefic;
-   functionally benefic;
-   functionally malefic;
-   or mixed for a particular Lagna.

This is critical.

For example, Jupiter is naturally benefic, but its functional role
changes according to Lagna and house ownership.

Therefore a simplistic rule such as:

> "Jupiter is benefic, so Jupiter always improves everything"

is not sufficient for classical Parashari analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 49. Exaltation and own-sign strength

Planetary dignity is central to Yoga analysis.

A planet can be:

-   exalted;
-   in own sign;
-   in Moolatrikona;
-   in a friend's sign;
-   neutral;
-   in an enemy's sign;
-   debilitated.

A Yoga requiring a strong planet should not be treated the same way when
that planet is severely weakened.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 50. Combustion

A planet close to the Sun may be considered **combust** under classical
rules.

This can reduce or modify its expression.

This is especially important for:

-   Mercury;
-   Venus;
-   Jupiter;
-   Saturn;
-   Mars.

Different traditions use different combustion thresholds.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 51. Retrogression

Retrograde motion (*vakri*) is interpreted differently across schools.

It can modify:

-   strength;
-   expression;
-   timing;
-   psychological experience;
-   repetition/delay themes.

Therefore a Yoga involving a retrograde planet deserves a separate
strength assessment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For each of the 12 houses, identify its lord.

**1, 4, 7, 10**

**1, 5, 9**

## Step 5 — Check Mahāpuruṣa Yogas

# 54. A useful hierarchy of evidence

When multiple Yoga interpretations conflict, use this hierarchy:

### Tier 1 — Exact classical formation

Does the actual rule exist?

### Tier 2 — Planetary strength

Are the participating planets strong?

### Tier 3 — House strength

Are the relevant houses strong?

### Tier 4 — Repetition

Is the same theme repeated elsewhere?

### Tier 5 — Dasha

Is the Yoga activated?

### Tier 6 — Transit

Is there current support?

This is more reliable within the Jyotisha framework than simply counting
named Yogas.

# 55. Why internet Yoga lists can be misleading

Modern websites often create lists containing:

-   classical Yogas;
-   regional names;
-   modern combinations;
-   duplicate names;
-   renamed Yogas;
-   different rules under the same name;
-   interpretations from later commentators.

Consequently, two websites may both claim to list "100 Yogas" while
having very different contents.

The safest practice is:

> **Always ask: Which text? Which chapter? Which verse? Which formation
> rule?**

# 58. The key answer to "how does a Yoga happen?"

A Yoga is created because **planetary geometry corresponds to a rule
defined by the Jyotisha tradition**.

For example:

### Mahāpuruṣa

Planet + Kendra + own/exalted sign.

### Gaja Kesari

Jupiter + Kendra relationship to Moon.

### Dhana

Wealth-house lords form specified relationships.

### Raja

Kendra/Trikona lords form specified relationships.

### Parivartana

Two planets exchange signs.

### Nābhasa

Seven planets form a specific geometric distribution.

So the "why" is not that a planet magically creates an event by itself.
Within the Jyotisha framework, the **relationship among significators is
interpreted as a symbolic configuration**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 59. A note about scientific status

Vedic astrology is a traditional astrological system, not a
scientifically established method for predicting future events.

The Yoga rules described here are therefore presented as **classical
Jyotisha doctrine**, not as scientifically demonstrated causal laws.

This distinction is especially important when applying astrology to:

-   medical decisions;
-   financial decisions;
-   legal matters;
-   major life decisions.

Astrology can be studied as a cultural, historical and interpretive
tradition, but it should not replace evidence-based professional advice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 60. Final conclusion

There is **no single authoritative total number of Vedic astrology
Yogas**.

A more accurate statement is:

> **Classical Jyotisha contains numerous Yoga systems. The Nābhasa
> system alone contains 32 named Yogas, while Parāśara and other
> classical authors describe many additional families including
> Mahāpuruṣa, Moon, Sun, Raja, Dhana, Dāridrya, Viparīta, Parivartana,
> Neecha-bhanga, Pravrajya and numerous special combinations.**

The most important skill is therefore not memorizing the largest
possible list.

It is learning to answer four questions:

1.  **Does the Yoga actually form according to the source's exact
    rule?**
2.  **How strong are the planets and houses involved?**
3.  **Is the Yoga modified, cancelled or contradicted by other
    factors?**
4.  **When is it activated by Dasha and transit?**

That approach produces a much more disciplined Jyotisha analysis than
simply counting Yoga names.

# Research note

The purpose of this document is to distinguish **classical source-based
Yoga definitions** from the much larger collection of combinations
circulated in modern astrology websites and software.

Where classical texts differ, the difference should be preserved rather
than silently merged into one "correct" rule.

**Recommended practice:** when a Yoga is identified in an individual
horoscope, record the **source + chapter/verse + exact formation rule**
before interpreting its result.
