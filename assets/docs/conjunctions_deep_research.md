# Vedic Astrology — Deep Research Report: Planetary Conjunctions by Bhāva, Rāśi and Lagna

**Research edition:** 3 October 2026  
**Framework:** Classical Parāśari Jyotiṣa with cross-reference to Phaladīpikā, Bṛhat Jātaka and Bṛhat Saṃhitā.

> **Methodological note:** Classical texts do not contain one modern table covering every planet pair in every house, sign and Ascendant. Where a rule is directly stated by a classical source it is identified as such; the 21-pair × 12-house × 12-sign framework is an organized interpretive synthesis built from classical principles.

## 1. The central rule

A conjunction should never be interpreted from the two planets alone. Use this sequence:

**Planet pair → Bhāva → Rāśi → dispositor → Lagna lordship → dignity → aspects → Vargas → Daśā → transit.**

Phaladīpikā explicitly recognizes two planets occupying the same house as one type of planetary connection, alongside sign exchange, mutual aspect, Kendra relation and Trikona relation. Its Chapter 18 then gives direct results for conjunctions of two planets and says that when more than two planets share a house, the several pairwise effects should be combined.

Its Bhāva methodology also stresses judging the Bhāva together with its lord and Karaka.

Bṛhat Jātaka has a dedicated chapter on planetary combinations, while Bṛhat Saṃhitā treats astronomical planetary meetings/Graha Yuddha separately.

### Classical pairwise observations

- **Sun–Moon:** identity and emotional mind become closely linked.
- **Sun–Mars:** authority, courage and action combine; the classical literature gives an energetic/forceful interpretation.
- **Sun–Mercury:** intellect, communication and administration; this is the basis of the commonly named Budha-Āditya combination, but the conjunction alone does not establish an exceptional result.
- **Sun–Jupiter:** authority, learning, counsel and principles.
- **Sun–Venus:** visibility, aesthetics, relationships and creative/luxury themes.
- **Sun–Saturn:** authority, duty, endurance and institutional structure.
- **Moon–Mars:** enterprise, initiative and emotional heat; associated with the Chandra-Maṅgala framework.
- **Moon–Mercury:** learning, speech, interpretation and adaptability.
- **Moon–Jupiter:** nurturing, learning, counsel and support; Gaja-Kesari is more specifically a Kendra relationship of Jupiter to Moon.
- **Moon–Venus:** aesthetics, relationships, comfort and artistic feeling.
- **Moon–Saturn:** responsibility, seriousness and endurance.
- **Mars–Mercury:** technical reasoning, strategy, debate, engineering/trading-type themes.
- **Mars–Jupiter:** leadership, initiative and strategic action guided by principles.
- **Mars–Venus:** passion, creativity, competition and desire.
- **Mars–Saturn:** force plus restraint; persistence, engineering and pressure tolerance.
- **Mercury–Jupiter:** scholarship, writing, advising, finance/law-type reasoning.
- **Mercury–Venus:** language, literature, design, music, negotiation and commerce.
- **Mercury–Saturn:** systems, calculation, technical work and disciplined communication.
- **Jupiter–Venus:** learning, wealth, arts and relationships; possible tension between principle and pleasure.
- **Jupiter–Saturn:** expansion plus structure; long-term planning and institution-building.
- **Venus–Saturn:** craftsmanship, disciplined creativity, durable resources and serious relationship expectations.

These are modern-neutral summaries of the classical pairwise material. Phaladīpikā Chapter 18 gives direct conjunction results for these pairs; its historical occupational and social wording should be understood in its period context.

## 10. The dispositor principle

A conjunction should always be followed to its sign lord.

Example: **Mars + Mercury in Libra**

1. Pair = action + intellect.
2. Libra = negotiation, contracts, balance, aesthetics.
3. Venus = dispositor; Venus's strength modifies the result.
4. House = tells where the combination operates.
5. Lagna = determines which houses Mars and Mercury own.

This is why two people with Mars–Mercury in Libra can have very different outcomes.

## 11. Strength modifiers

For each conjunction inspect:

- own sign;
- Mūlatrikoṇa;
- exaltation;
- debilitation;
- friendly/enemy sign;
- Shadbala;
- combustion;
- retrogression;
- planetary war where technically applicable;
- benefic/malefic aspects;
- dispositor strength;
- Nakṣatra and Nakṣatra lord;
- Navāṃśa;
- relevant Varga;
- Daśā;
- transit.

Phaladīpikā's Bhāva methodology specifically emphasizes the Bhāva, Bhāva lord, Karaka, association/aspect and planetary strength rather than a single placement.

## 12. Sun conjunctions: special caution

Sun conjunctions require degree-level checking because combustion may modify Mercury, Venus and other planets. A same-sign conjunction is not automatically identical to a close conjunction.

Do not treat every Sun–Mercury conjunction as an extraordinary Budha-Āditya Yoga without checking strength, house, sign, dispositor and affliction.

## 13. Rahu and Ketu conjunctions

Rahu/Ketu should be analyzed through:
- sign and dispositor;
- house;
- associated planet;
- Nakṣatra;
- aspects/relationships;
- Dasha.

Phaladīpikā specifically discusses circumstances in which Rahu/Ketu in Kendra/Trikona and connected with relevant lords can behave auspiciously, illustrating why node conjunctions cannot be reduced to a simple “malefic + planet” formula.

## 14. Multi-planet conjunctions

If n planets occupy one sign/house, the number of unique pairs is **n(n−1)/2**.

| Planets | Pair relationships |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 6 | 15 |
| 7 | 21 |

Phaladīpikā explicitly says that when more than two planets occupy a house, combine the effects of the possible pairs.

But also identify the dominant planet, house lord, dispositor, closest degrees and repeated theme. Otherwise a multi-planet conjunction becomes an unmanageable list of contradictory keywords.

## 15. Graha Yuddha is not ordinary yuti

Bṛhat Saṃhitā describes several types of planetary meeting and the idea of one planet being defeated in a planetary encounter. This is an astronomical/technical layer and should not be declared simply because two planets share a sign.

## 16. Daśā and activation

A conjunction may exist throughout the birth chart but become prominent during the Mahādaśā/Antardaśā of:

- either participating planet;
- the dispositor;
- an important house lord involved in the conjunction;
- or another strongly connected planet.

This timing layer explains why the same natal combination can manifest at different ages.

## 17. Navāṃśa and other Vargas

For advanced analysis, verify the conjunction in the relevant divisional charts. D-9 is particularly important for planetary strength and relationship/dharma themes; D-10 is especially relevant when the conjunction is being interpreted for profession.

A repeated theme across D-1 and an appropriate Varga is generally more informative within the Jyotiṣa framework than an isolated D-1 keyword.

## 18. Worked synthesis

### Mars + Mercury in Virgo, 10th house, Gemini Lagna

**Pair:** technical intelligence, strategy, communication and execution.

**House:** career, public action and reputation.

**Sign:** Virgo emphasizes precision, analysis, service and systems.

**Lagna lordship:** Mercury owns the 1st and 4th for Gemini; Mars owns the 6th and 11th.

Therefore the conjunction links identity/foundations with competition/service, gains/networks and profession. A full judgment would then inspect Mercury's dignity, Mars's dignity, dispositor condition, aspects, combustion, D-9/D-10 and active Daśā.

This example demonstrates the main principle: **a conjunction has no complete interpretation until the Lagna-specific lordship is added.**

## 19. Common errors

### Error 1 — Treating a conjunction as automatically good or bad
A conjunction is a relationship, not a verdict.

### Error 2 — Ignoring house ownership
The same pair performs different functional jobs under different Lagnas.

### Error 3 — Calling every Moon–Jupiter conjunction Gaja-Kesari
The classical Gaja-Kesari framework is specifically based on Jupiter's Kendra relationship to Moon.

### Error 4 — Calling every Sun–Mercury conjunction exceptional
Degree separation, combustion, dignity, house and dispositor matter.

### Error 5 — Treating three planets as one simple Yoga
Three planets contain three pair relationships before multi-planet synthesis.

### Error 6 — Ignoring the dispositor
The sign lord can materially strengthen, redirect or constrain the conjunction.

## 21. Final conclusion

The most useful way to study conjunctions is not as a dictionary of fixed predictions but as a **three-dimensional matrix**:

**Planet pair × Bhāva × Rāśi**, followed by **Lagna-specific lordship and timing**.

The classical texts support this layered approach: conjunction is one type of planetary connection; Bhāva judgment requires the Bhāva, its lord and Karaka; pairwise conjunction results are treated separately; and Daśā/transit material provides activation and timing.
